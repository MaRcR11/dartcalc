dartcalc

Every way to check out a score in darts with a double-out. The last dart must hit a double or the bullseye, and the order of the darts matters. Missed darts aren't counted.

Checkouts per score

Bars are colored by the fewest darts needed to finish. Gray dots under the axis mark bogey numbers. Click a bar to list its checkouts.

The formula behind the chart

The two polynomials

Tag every dart field with a term x raised to its value and add them up. All 62 fields give A(x); the 21 finishing fields (doubles + bull) give F(x):

A(x) = x1 + 2x2 + 2x3 + … + x60 F(x) = x2 + x4 + … + x40 + x50

Counting one score

The number of ways to check out s with up to n darts is the coefficient of xs in a single product:

F(x) · ( 1 + A(x) + A(x)2 + … + A(x)n−1 )

Each power of A is one more dart convolved onto the last, which is why the bars pile into a bell.

The grand total

Set x = 1 and the whole thing collapses to the grand total: F(1) = 21, A(1) = 62, so there are 21 · (62n − 1) / 61 checkouts in all, which is 82,047 for 3 darts.

Bogey numbers

Write maxn = 60(n−1) + 50 for the highest checkout with n darts. Near the top you can only lose points in steps of 3 (triple to triple), 10 (a bull), or an even amount (the finishing double), and 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11 are not sums of those. Those steps do not depend on n: an extra dart just adds a T20, lifting maxn by 60 and leaving the top untouched. So the bogey numbers (coefficient 0) are, for every n ≥ 2 out to infinity, exactly maxn − {1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11}.

Only one way

The only-one-way scores (coefficient 1) are just as fixed: for every n ≥ 3 they are exactly {2, 3, maxn − 14, maxn}. At the floor 2 = D1 and 3 = S1, D1; at the ceiling maxn = (n−1)×T20 + Bull and maxn − 14 = (n−1)×T20 + D18.
